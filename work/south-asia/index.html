Economic Growth and Women’s Employment in South Asia

Descriptive endpoint comparison of Pakistan, Bangladesh and India, 2010–2024.

The research question

How did economic growth and women’s employment indicators change across three South Asian economies between 2010 and 2024? This descriptive public-data demonstration compares Pakistan, Bangladesh and India to make differences in economic and employment indicators readable.

Data and analytical scope

The supplied public-data work sample references World Bank World Development Indicators: GDP per capita in constant 2015 US dollars, and the female employment-to-population ratio, ages 15+, using modelled ILO estimates. The website comparison uses 2010 and 2024 endpoints. The PDF describes annual source data with 45 country-years and 135 source values, all requested values present, and no imputation or smoothing. These are reported dataset characteristics, not independently verified counts on this website. The comparison is not a population-weighted regional average.

Methods are descriptive: percentage change in real GDP per capita, endpoint comparison of female employment ratios, and employment-ratio differences in percentage points. No regressions, causal identification or intermediate annual observations are presented.

Bangladesh has the largest supplied increase in real GDP per capita (105.72%), followed by India (91.62%) and Pakistan (30.11%). Female employment ratios rise by 3.203, 4.852 and 0.333 percentage points respectively. India has the largest employment-ratio increase among the three countries, while Bangladesh has the highest female employment ratio at both supplied endpoints.

What this comparison cannot establish

Endpoint differences do not show the path between 2010 and 2024, the drivers of change or a causal relationship between growth and employment. Modelled estimates have methodological limitations. The supplied PDF documents retrieval on 14 October 2026 and a World Bank API database update on 8 October 2026. Raw API responses and the companion dataset were not supplied here, so the website presents verified endpoint summaries and does not reconstruct annual observations.

Contribution and available samples

This public-data portfolio exercise was prepared for Asif Hussain’s review with AI assistance, as disclosed in the supplied PDF. It is not previously published research or commissioned client work. The PDF reports executed and checked Python calculations, while its Stata script requires review and execution in Stata. Those scripts and raw source files are not supplied here; the website does not claim an independent rerun or sole authorship by Asif Hussain.

Source detail and interpretationWDI indicator codes: NY.GDP.PCAP.KD (GDP per capita); SL.EMP.TOTL.SP.FE.ZS (female employment ratio); SL.EMP.TOTL.SP.MA.ZS (male employment ratio). The PDF reports rounded 2024 male–female gaps of 53.9, 40.2 and 43.2 percentage points for Pakistan, Bangladesh and India. GDP per capita is an average output measure, not median household income, and constant-dollar values are not PPP-adjusted. The age-15+ denominator can be affected by demographic change.

Source document reviewed privately. Download withheld pending sharing approval.
